How EMI Is Calculated

Every EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is calculated using a fixed reducing-balance formula. It keeps your monthly payment constant while the split between principal and interest changes every month.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]

  • P — Principal (effective loan amount after down payment)
  • r — Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n — Number of monthly installments (tenure in months)

Worked Example

Loan of ₹10,00,000 at 8.5% p.a. for 20 years (240 months):

  • Monthly rate, r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083
  • EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.007083 × (1.007083)240 ÷ [(1.007083)240 − 1]
  • EMI ≈ ₹8,678 / month
  • Total paid over 20 years ≈ ₹20,82,720, of which ≈ ₹10,82,720 is interest

Factors That Affect Your EMI

Principal Amount

A larger loan amount directly increases your EMI, assuming rate and tenure stay the same.

Interest Rate

Even a small change in rate compounds over the loan tenure and can shift your EMI noticeably.

Loan Tenure

A longer tenure lowers your EMI but increases the total interest you pay over the loan's life.

Down Payment

Paying more upfront reduces the principal being financed, lowering both EMI and total interest.

Benefits of Prepayment

Less Total Interest

Extra payments reduce your outstanding principal early, so future interest is calculated on a smaller balance.

Shorter Tenure

Consistent prepayments can shave months, even years, off your original loan schedule.

Debt-Free Sooner

Closing a loan earlier frees up your monthly cash flow for savings or other investments.

Lower Lifetime Cost

The earlier a prepayment is made, the more interest it saves — time matters as much as amount.

Tips to Reduce Your EMI

  1. Make a larger down payment to shrink the principal being financed.
  2. Compare interest rates across lenders before committing — a lower rate compounds savings.
  3. Opt for a longer tenure only if you genuinely need a lower monthly outflow; it costs more in total interest.
  4. Make small, regular prepayments whenever you have surplus cash.
  5. Maintain a strong credit profile — it is one of the biggest levers for negotiating a better rate.
  6. Refinance or transfer your loan if a significantly lower rate becomes available elsewhere.
  7. Avoid stretching your EMI beyond roughly 40% of your monthly income.

Did You Know?

In the early years of a loan, most of your EMI goes toward interest, not principal — that only flips later in the tenure.
Paying just one extra EMI a year on a 20-year home loan can shorten the tenure by several years.
A 1% drop in interest rate on a large, long-tenure loan can save an amount comparable to a small car.
Prepaying early in the loan life saves far more interest than prepaying the same amount later.
EMI as a concept became widespread only once amortization tables could be computed at scale — today your browser does it instantly.
